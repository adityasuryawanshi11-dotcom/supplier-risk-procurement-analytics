How the model works

From purchase orders to risk

  1. Load the fictional supplier and purchase-order CSVs into an in-memory SQLite database.
  2. Aggregate purchase-order counts, late deliveries, units, defects, and actual and contracted spend in SQL.
  3. Convert each metric to a comparable 0–100 risk input using fixed illustrative thresholds.
  4. Multiply each input by its weight and add the results. A higher score indicates more modeled exposure.

Low: below 30 · Medium: 30 to below 60 · High: 60 or above. The score is not a probability and has not been validated on real supplier outcomes.

Default scoring inputs

30% · DeliveryLate PO % scaled to 100 at 30%.
25% · QualityDefective-unit % scaled to 100 at 3%.
20% · CostPositive cost variance scaled to 100 at 10%.
15% · GeographyFictional scenario input from 0–100.
10% · ConcentrationCategory spend share scaled from 25% to 60%.

Every component is capped at 100. Thresholds and geographic values are examples, not industry standards.

Data & limitations

This demo contains 15 invented suppliers and 900 completed POs. Cost variance is calculated as (actual spend − contracted spend) ÷ contracted spend. A late PO is delivered after its promised date; the defect rate uses total defective units ÷ total units. Spend excludes taxes, freight, and other total-cost factors.

Category share always uses the complete category's spend as its denominator, even when region filters are applied. The recommendation text is rule-based; AI-generated text is only produced when explicitly requested.
